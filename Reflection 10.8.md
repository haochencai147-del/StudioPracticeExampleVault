First, in terms of research, I’ve come to realize that it’s not merely about finding theories or artists to use as "references" for my work, but rather about situating my practice within a broader artistic and cultural context. Research helps me understand the relationship between my work and both historical and contemporary practices, while also enabling me to develop a more precise critical vocabulary and further clarify the uniqueness of my work.

One approach that has been particularly helpful for me is starting research with keywords and mind maps. Instead of directly searching on broad topics, breaking down my interests into more specific keywords—such as medium, technique, theoretical frameworks, social contexts, and geographical backgrounds—makes it easier to locate materials truly relevant to my work. The mapping exercises in class also helped me realize that different interests can be interconnected; through continuous categorization, linking, and reorganization, my research direction gradually becomes clearer.

Another crucial aspect is how to give and receive feedback. In the past, I might have understood feedback simply as others telling me “what’s good or bad” about my work. However, through classmates presenting their works and engaging in discussions, I’ve realized that the purpose of feedback isn’t necessarily to “correct” the work, but rather to help creators gain a clearer understanding of their work’s current ambitions and concerns. I now see this kind of interaction as part of the research process itself. Misunderstandings, alternative interpretations, or new associations that others bring up aren’t necessarily negative—they actually reveal aspects of my work that I hadn’t previously recognized.
